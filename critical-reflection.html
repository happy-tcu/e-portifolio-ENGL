Assignment 04

Critical Reflection

A semester of working one argument — the AI Divide — across four different forms.

This semester gave me the opportunity to explore a topic of my choice, the AI Divide, across four very different forms: a genre analysis, a research report, a visual argument, and a set of reading responses. Working with the same case study across so many modes taught me that an argument is not something you write once and leave unchanged. Instead, it is a claim you continually reshape for different audiences and formats. Each version reveals what is truly load-bearing and what was only scaffolding.

The research report forced me to be evidence-honest. The poster forced me to be selective. The genre analysis forced me to read other writers the way a writer reads.

The skill I most want to keep developing is writing across modes: translating the same idea for a skimmer, a close reader, and a viewer who may only engage with the image. That is the toolkit I need for the policy and advocacy work I want to do beyond TCU, where the strength of an argument matters less if the right person never encounters it in a form they are willing to read.

Critical reflection — ENGL 20803-010, Texas Christian University.
