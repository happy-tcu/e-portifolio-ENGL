Assignment 04

Critical Reflection

A short note on the visual-argument poster — what worked, what slowed me down, and what I want to keep developing.

I think I met the poster requirements, both based on the instructions and the peer feedback from my classmates. My writing background helped a lot, especially during the ideation and drafting stages, where I drew on my speech and debate experience to build a visual argument and tell a compelling story. What slowed me down was the opposite of writer's block — I had too much to say, and every design pass meant deciding what to cut so the poster could breathe. That became my biggest takeaway: a visual argument is not a shorter essay. It works by selection and emphasis, and what you leave out shapes the argument as much as what you include. Moving forward, I am fascinated by the idea of multimodality and want to keep developing the discipline of writing for a skimmer first and a close reader second — a skill that carries well beyond this class into the policy and advocacy work I want to do.

Critical reflection — ENGL 20803-010, Texas Christian University.
